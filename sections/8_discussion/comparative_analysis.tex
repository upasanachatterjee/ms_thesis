\section{Specialized Transformers versus Large Language Models}
\label{sec:comparative-analysis}

\subsection{Performance Characteristics and Trade-offs}

Our experiments reveal distinct performance profiles between domain-adapted transformer architectures and fine-tuned large language models, and the differences bear directly on practical deployment and system-design choices.

The best-performing specialized transformer configuration ($T_{id}(32) + T_h$) reaches 62.19 F1-Macro on the AllSides Media split through continued pre-training with metalinguistic objectives, substantially exceeding zero-shot GPT-4o-mini at 51.86 F1-Macro. The specialized model requires no labeled training data beyond the contrastive triplets derived from source-level annotations, and in this sense it is markedly more data-efficient than an LLM-based pipeline that depends on labelled fine-tuning examples.

Fine-tuned GPT-4o-mini, trained on 2,000 labelled examples, reaches 72.48 F1-Macro on the Media split and so surpasses our best specialized transformer by 10.29 points. On the Media split this is a meaningful gap in absolute terms, and when headline classification accuracy is the primary criterion the fine-tuned LLM is the stronger model. Two qualifications matter for interpreting that gap. First, on the AllSidesV2 Random split our fine-tuned RoBERTa reaches F1 = 90.91, substantially above the fine-tuned GPT result, so the specialized encoder is not generally the weaker model; it is specifically weaker on the source-controlled split where the task is hardest. Second, the Media-split advantage carries several methodological costs that the specialized pipeline avoids: fine-tuning an LLM requires substantial labelled data, and the opacity of its pre-training corpus raises data-contamination concerns that do not arise under our controlled transformer pre-training.

\subsection{Classification Performance Asymmetries}

Both methodologies exhibit systematic asymmetries across ideological categories that warrant caution in real-world deployment. Neither approach achieves balanced performance across Left, Center, and Right, and both struggle particularly with centrist and right-leaning content. Classifiers that systematically mischaracterise these categories risk reinforcing rather than mitigating polarisation effects in downstream applications.

The gains from metalinguistic objectives are concentrated precisely on these difficult categories, which provides empirical support for the view that stylistic and affective dimensions contribute discriminative information that content-based representations do not fully capture. This accords with theoretical frameworks from political communication research that emphasise the multi-dimensional nature of ideological expression in media discourse~\citep{hamborg2019automatedmediabias}.

\subsection{Computational and Economic Considerations}

The choice between specialized transformers and fine-tuned LLMs also involves substantial trade-offs in computational cost, operational complexity, and transparency. Specialized transformers with metalinguistic objectives offer competitive accuracy at moderate computational expense, with full architectural transparency and superior data efficiency, which makes them well suited to deployments that prioritise interpretability, cost control, or trust.

Fine-tuned LLMs can achieve higher raw performance when labelled data and compute are available, but practitioners must weigh several limiting factors: substantial fine-tuning and inference costs, potential data-contamination effects from opaque pre-training, reduced interpretability, and vendor-dependency risks for API-based pipelines~\citep{Wen_2023}.

The comparison above turns on classification accuracy, and accuracy alone is a limited lens on the question of which family of models should be preferred. Two classifiers with similar F1 can assign the same label to an article for quite different reasons, and the reasons begin to matter when the distribution of test articles shifts, or when the downstream application depends on the causal basis of the label rather than only its value. The causal analysis that follows re-examines the same specialized transformer and GPT paradigms in pathway terms, asking not only whether they agree with human annotators on the label but whether they arrive at that label through comparable textual channels.
